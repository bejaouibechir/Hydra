This week I submitted Hydra ETL to 4 "awesome" lists on GitHub.

These lists are curated by volunteers: awesome-data-engineering, awesome-pipeline, awesome-duckdb, awesome-mcp-servers. Thousands of developers browse them to pick their tools.

What I learned by reading their rules before submitting:

→ Some refuse beta projects. awesome-python only accepts "production-ready" tools. I'll wait for v1.0.
→ Others won't list a tool submitted by its own author until it has outside users. Fair enough.
→ One MCP list requires the server to pass an automated check before it's accepted.

Lesson: an open-source project earns visibility with proof, not announcements.

Next step: find 3 teams willing to run Hydra ETL on a real pipeline. If you move data between files and databases (CSV, Parquet, PostgreSQL, MySQL…) and your scripts are getting hard to read, send me a message.
